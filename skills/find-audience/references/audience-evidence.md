# Choosing evidence for specialist audiences

Use this guidance when a request depends on a professional role, specialist knowledge or demonstrated task ability. It applies across available Domain Expert pools, including programming, healthcare, STEM, languages, finance and legal. Discover what the workspace actually exposes; this reference is not a catalogue of available filters or IDs.

## Match the evidence to the requirement

- **Self-reported occupation:** profile answers about a job role or job title establish the participant's reported occupation. They do not establish assessed proficiency or independently checked credentials unless a supported definition explicitly says so.
- **Self-reported proficiency:** profile answers about programming languages or other skills establish claimed proficiency. They are distinct from Domain Expert qualifications, even when the labels use the same language or discipline.
- **Checked credentials or experience:** independent checks can support professional standing or relevant experience. Explain the documented check, such as a licence check or professional-profile match; these establish different things.
- **Assessed domain skills:** an assessment can establish demonstrated knowledge or ability in its tested domain. Explain its documented scope, without inventing scores, thresholds, dates or assurance about a different task.
- **Assessed task skills:** task qualifications support the ability they assess. For example, AI Tasker qualification does not by itself establish expertise in medicine or programming.

Recommend the strongest relevant evidence for what participants need to do. For professional standing, consider checked credentials; for specialist knowledge, assessed domain skills; for a specific activity, relevant assessed task skills. Require a combination only when both requirements are necessary. Preserve explicit must-haves and maximise reach within the best-supported audience.

For requests such as coding evaluation, clinical judgement or specialist translation, actively search for relevant Domain Expert filters alongside ordinary profile filters before recommending an audience. For research about someone's working life, occupation may be the relevant selection. Do not add expert restrictions to general tasks without a reason.

## Documented Domain Expert context

Prolific's [Domain Experts guidance](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/445228-domain-experts) describes verified specialist pools and field-dependent verification through skills assessments, credentials and experience checks. Its [verification overview](https://www.prolific.com/resources/introducing-domain-experts-evaluate-and-annotate-your-ai-with-verified-experts) explains that programmers solve coding challenges in their claimed languages, language specialists undergo fluency testing, healthcare professionals undergo licence documentation and clinical checks, and STEM experts use field-specific tests and academic verification.

An externally selectable programming-language filter explicitly categorised as **Domain Experts** should therefore be considered as a skills-assessed programming audience, distinct from the ordinary self-reported **Programming Languages** filter. Sparse CLI descriptions do not erase that documented distinction. Apply the same approach to other domains using the documented method for that pool, rather than calling every Domain Expert uniformly skills-assessed.

Distinguish what the public programme documentation establishes from what the individual filter exposes. Use live catalogue results for filter identity, category and selections. Do not infer the exact assessment provider, current credential status, score, recency or a verification level that is not exposed or documented. A label or model confidence alone is not verification evidence.

## Ambiguity, alternatives and missing evidence

Infer the appropriate evidence from the task rather than routinely asking whether the user wants verified experts. Ask a short clarification only when credible interpretations would materially change who qualifies, such as studying a profession versus testing specialist ability, or when the relevant specialism is unresolved. Retrieve the candidate filters and counts that make that decision answerable.

Explain meaningful alternatives in plain language. Count complete audiences separately and count any proposed OR union directly, subject to workspace rules. Do not require a self-reported job role in addition to an expert qualification unless current occupation is also essential. Preserve qualification requirements when a count is low or privacy-floored; explain the limitation rather than silently substituting self-report.

Use only externally selectable filters available in the workspace. If a required qualification is unavailable or its meaning cannot be established, explain what remains unchecked. Custom screening requires separate setup and must not be presented as an existing Prolific qualification.
