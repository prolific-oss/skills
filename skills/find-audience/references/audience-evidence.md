# Audience qualification evidence

## What the evidence establishes

| Evidence | Establishes | Does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-reported occupation | A claimed job role | Checked credentials or assessed ability |
| Self-reported proficiency | Claimed skill or fluency | Assessed proficiency |
| Checked credentials or experience | The professional standing or experience actually checked | Assessed domain or task ability; a profile match is not a licence check |
| Assessed domain skills | Ability within the assessed domain | Ability on a different task |
| Assessed task skills | Ability on the assessed activity | Specialist domain knowledge; AI Tasker qualification is not medical or programming expertise |

## Documented Domain Expert methods

Prolific's programme documentation describes field-dependent verification:

- Programming: coding challenges in the claimed programming language.
- Languages: fluency testing.
- Healthcare: licence documentation and clinical checks.
- STEM: field-specific tests and academic verification.
- Other pools, including finance and legal: use the documented definition for that pool; do not
  assume the methods above apply.

Use these programme-level facts alongside the live filter's identity, category and choices. Sparse
filter descriptions do not erase the distinction between Domain Experts and self-report. The
Domain Experts category does not imply one uniform assessment or verification method.
Use only externally selectable qualifications exposed in the workspace.

Explain the documented scope. Do not invent individual scores, thresholds, providers, assessment
dates, current licence status or guarantees of performance on the requested task. A category label
or model confidence alone does not establish those details.
